\section{Introduction}

Human--Camera generation requires both modality quality and cross-modal coherence. Symmetric
joint modeling couples these goals, but it also makes the Human generator mutable under Camera
inputs and supervision. We study a narrower question: can Camera generation be added to a
strong Human generator while keeping the finalized Human pathway unchanged?

\storymotion{} uses a directed factorization. A frozen Human generator first owns Human motion
generation. One Camera conditional function then operates on either observed Human motion or a
Human sample generated in a first pass. Generated-Human Human--Camera generation is therefore
an explicit two-pass composition, not synchronous joint sampling.

Our bounded contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A capability-preserving asymmetric design in which Camera training cannot rewrite the
  finalized Human generator, together with three complementary evaluation interfaces.
  \item A human-owned, relation-aware representation with an explicit interaction channel and a
  protected dual-stream generator. We do not claim semantic disentanglement or universal
  component necessity.
  \item A field-wise evaluation and artifact protocol that separates Human generation,
  observed-Human Camera generation, and generated-Human two-pass generation, including explicit
  uncertainty and metric-semantics boundaries.
  \item \evidencepending: an audited Camera-text response and matched Camera CFG study. This
  contribution will be removed if its preregistered gates fail.
\end{itemize}

We do not claim the first Human--Camera generator, a model-agnostic framework, arbitrary
editing, online generation, real-time performance, or global superiority over symmetric joint
modeling.
