\section{Problem Definition and Evaluation Interfaces}

Let $T_H$ and $T_C$ denote Human and Camera descriptions, respectively. \storymotion{} uses
two conditional distributions:
\begin{align}
  \hat H &\sim p_H(H\mid T_H), \\
  \hat C &\sim p_C(C\mid H,T_C).
\end{align}
Their directed composition defines
\begin{equation}
  p(H,C\mid T_H,T_C)=p_H(H\mid T_H)p_C(C\mid H,T_C).
\end{equation}

We evaluate three interfaces:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Human generation:} $T_H\rightarrow H$.
  \item \textbf{Observed-H Camera generation:} $H_{\mathrm{obs}}+T_C\rightarrow C$.
  \item \textbf{Generated-H Human--Camera generation:} first sample $\hat H$ and keep it fixed,
  then sample $\hat C\sim p_C(C\mid\hat H,T_C)$.
\end{enumerate}

The third interface contains Human generation error and a generated-Human condition shift. Its
Camera deviation from the factual dataset Camera is a dataset-target diagnostic, not distance
to a unique valid Camera trajectory.
